How PlainArmor Works in 3 Steps

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026 · 4 min read

PlainArmor is a US-only service that helps small businesses stop strangers from sending email that looks like it came from their own domain. The process has three steps: we check your public DNS, you see a mock of the problem, and you follow step-by-step instructions yourself. This guide walks through each one, including what happens behind the scenes and what we do not do.

Step 1: We check your public DNS

You give us your company's domain. We look up its public DNS records, the same information every receiving mail server reads when a message arrives. We are looking mainly for a DMARC record, and for what it tells receivers to do with mail that fails authentication.

This step needs no password, no login and no software. We never access your mailboxes, your contacts or your files. Because the records are public, the check is a read-only look at information anyone could retrieve.

The result usually falls into one of three groups. Your domain may have no DMARC record at all. It may have one set to p=none, which only monitors. Or it may already have a policy of quarantine or reject. The first two leave a gap: nothing instructs receivers to refuse a message that falsely uses your domain in the From address.

Step 2: You see the mock fake email

If there is a gap, we show you a mock fake email on screen. It is an example of what a stranger could try: a message that appears to come from your company. It is drawn on the page so you can understand the problem in concrete terms.

Nothing is sent. The mock is never delivered to your inbox, your staff or anyone else, and we never send fake emails. You will not find it in any mailbox, so there is nothing to explain to your team or clean up afterward.

At this point you can decide whether you want to fix the gap. If you do not, you have lost nothing but a few minutes.

Step 3: You follow the steps yourself

Fix it is a one-time purchase of step-by-step instructions, $199. You follow them yourself at your DNS host, the service where your domain's records are managed. We never touch your DNS.

There are two routes, matched to how your business sends email:

  • The fast route is for businesses where no other services send email as the domain. It takes about 20 days to reach the strictest setting, p=reject.
  • The careful staged route is for businesses where other services, such as billing, newsletter or customer-management tools, send email as you. It has checkpoints at days 7, 14, 21 and 28, and always checks that your mail still works first.

If you are unsure which applies, the staged route is the safer choice, and the instructions help you find out which services send as you.

Why it is done in stages

A strict DMARC policy tells receivers to refuse mail that fails authentication. That is the goal, but if a legitimate sender was missed, its mail can be refused too. Moving in stages, with checks that mail still works, protects you from that outcome. If something does go wrong, rollback is one step: you restore the earlier setting at your DNS host.

What happens afterward

When you finish, your domain publishes a policy asking receivers to reject fake mail that uses your exact domain. This makes that kind of spoofing much harder, but it does not stop lookalike domains or hacked mailboxes, which are different problems. Our guide on what we do not protect against covers them honestly.

One optional follow-on exists: a quarterly re-test at $99 a year, billed yearly and renewing until you cancel. It is not required. Support replies within one business day, and all sales are final.

Frequently asked questions

Do you send a real fake email to test me? No. The fake email is a mock on screen and is never sent.

Do you log in to my DNS? No. We never touch your DNS or settings. You make every change.

How long does it take? The fast route takes about 20 days; the staged route has checkpoints at days 7, 14, 21 and 28.

What if my email stops working? Undo the last change. Rollback is one step.
