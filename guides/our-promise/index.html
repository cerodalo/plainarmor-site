Our Promise: What PlainArmor Does and Never Does

Trust matters more when the subject is email security, so here is what PlainArmor does, what it never does, and where its limits are, written as plainly as we can.

What we do

We check your company's public DNS records to see whether anything stops a stranger from sending an email that looks like it came from your domain. If there is a gap, we show you an on-screen mock of what that fake could look like. If you choose to buy Fix it, we give you step-by-step instructions that you follow yourself. We answer support questions within one business day, and all sales are final.

What we never do

We never send fake emails. The fake email you see is a mock drawn on a web page. It is never delivered to your inbox, your staff, your customers or anyone else. No message leaves PlainArmor pretending to be you.

We never touch your DNS. We do not log in to your registrar, DNS host or email provider, and we never ask for those passwords. You make every change yourself, or hand the steps to someone you trust. If anyone claiming to be PlainArmor asks for your passwords, treat it as suspicious.

We never read your email. We have no access to your mailboxes, contacts or files.

We never change your settings. The instructions tell you what to do. Whether and when you do it is your decision.

What we read

Only public DNS records, which are available to anyone on the internet. These are the same records that receiving mail systems look up every time a message arrives. Reading them requires no permission from you and gives us no access to anything private.

What we do not promise

We make no guarantees, because an honest one is not possible.

  • A strong DMARC setting makes it much harder for strangers to send mail that looks like it is from your exact domain. It does not make impersonation impossible.
  • It does not stop lookalike domains, which are separate domains owned by someone else.
  • It does not stop display-name tricks, where only the sender's name resembles someone you know.
  • It does not stop a hacked mailbox, because mail sent from a genuinely compromised account is real mail from your systems.
  • A strict setting can block your own legitimate mail if a sender is missing. That is why the instructions check that mail still works first, and why rollback is one step.

For more detail on these limits, see our guide on what PlainArmor does not protect against.

What we ask of you

Follow the steps in order, tell us if something on your screen does not match, and keep a note of which services send email as your company. If something breaks, undo the last change, which is one step, and contact support.

Our pricing is the pricing

The prices are the ones listed on our pricing page: Fix it is $199 one time, and the optional quarterly re-test is $99 per year, billed yearly and renewing until canceled. There are no hidden fees from PlainArmor.

Who we serve

PlainArmor serves US small businesses only. We offer practical technical instructions, not legal advice. If you need to understand legal or regulatory obligations, please speak with a qualified professional.
