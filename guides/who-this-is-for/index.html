PlainArmor is built for a specific kind of business, and being clear about who that is helps you decide whether it fits. In short: US small businesses, roughly one to fifty people, with a company domain and email but no dedicated IT person.

A typical PlainArmor customer

You run an accounting practice, a contractor, a clinic, a law office, a shop, a small agency or a nonprofit. You have a company email address on your own domain. Someone, maybe you, sets up email, the website and the occasional tool, but nobody has the title of IT manager.

You have heard that someone can send email that looks like it came from your company, and you would like to know whether that is true for you, and what to do about it, without wading through jargon.

Why small businesses

Smaller businesses are often well known and trusted by a small circle of customers and vendors, and they rarely have anyone watching email security day to day. A message that appears to come from your company can carry real weight with the people who know you. At the same time, setting up DMARC properly can feel technical, and a careless attempt can block your own mail. PlainArmor gives plain-English steps, in an order that protects your mail while you go.

What you need to bring

  • A company domain. The part after the at sign in your email address.
  • Access to where your DNS is managed. That is usually your registrar, website host or a separate DNS service. If someone else manages it, you can pass them the steps.
  • A little time. The fast route takes about 20 days to reach the strictest setting, and the staged route has checkpoints at days 7, 14, 21 and 28. Most of that is waiting, not working.
  • A list of services that send email as you, or willingness to find out. The instructions help.

What you do not need

You do not need to be technical, to install software, to give us a password, or to have an IT person. We never log in to your accounts or touch your DNS, and we never send fake emails.

Who it may not suit

Be realistic about fit. PlainArmor may not be right if you:

  • Want someone else to make the changes for you. We sell instructions, not hands-on service.
  • Need broad security services such as device protection, mailbox monitoring or staff training. We address one narrow problem.
  • Are outside the United States. We serve US businesses only.
  • Run a large organization with a dedicated team and complex email setup. You likely have in-house expertise.

What it costs

Fix it is $199 one time and all sales are final. The only add-on is an optional quarterly re-test at $99 a year, billed yearly. Details are on the pricing page.

What it does and does not do

It makes it much harder for strangers to send mail that looks like it came from your exact domain. It does not stop lookalike domains, display-name tricks or hacked mailboxes, and it is not legal advice. Our promise page explains what we do and never do.

Frequently asked questions

Is PlainArmor only for US businesses? Yes.

How big a company is it for? Roughly one to fifty people.

Do I need an IT person? No, but you need to log in to where your DNS is managed, or hand the steps to whoever does.

Who might it not suit? Businesses that want the changes made for them, or that need wider security services.
