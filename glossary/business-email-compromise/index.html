What Is Business Email Compromise (BEC)?

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026

Business email compromise, often shortened to BEC, is fraud in which someone poses as a trusted person, such as an owner, vendor or customer, to get money or sensitive information sent to the wrong place. A common version is a message that changes the bank details on an invoice.

It matters to small businesses because payments are often handled by a few people by email, with little formal verification. BEC can use a spoofed address, a lookalike domain or a genuinely hacked mailbox, so DMARC helps with only one of those routes.

What to do: confirm any payment or bank-detail change by phone using a number you already have, require two people for large payments where possible, and enforce DMARC to close off exact-domain spoofing.
