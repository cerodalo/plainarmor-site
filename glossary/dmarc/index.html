What Is DMARC?

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026

DMARC is a DNS record that tells receiving mail systems what to do with messages that claim to come from your domain but fail authentication. It ties SPF and DKIM to the From address people see, and lets you request reports about who is sending mail as you.

For a small business, DMARC is the setting that turns email authentication into real protection against exact-domain spoofing. Without a firm policy, each receiver decides for itself. DMARC does not stop lookalike domains or hacked mailboxes, and a strict policy can block your own legitimate mail if a sender is missing.

What to do: publish a DMARC record, start by monitoring, confirm all your senders pass, then move to quarantine and finally reject.
