Email Security Glossary

Email security is full of acronyms, and most definitions assume you already know the field. These entries do not. Each term gets a plain-English definition first, then a short note on why it matters to a small business, and one line on what to do about it. They cover threats like spoofing and phishing, authentication settings like SPF, DKIM and DMARC, the DNS basics behind them, and everyday terms like deliverability. Nothing here is legal advice, and PlainArmor serves US businesses only.

FHSW

F

From Address vs Return-Path: What Is the Difference?
The From address is what people see; the Return-Path is a hidden bounce address SPF checks. Why the difference matters for spoofing and DMARC.

H

How Does Email Forwarding Affect DMARC?
Forwarded email can fail SPF because it comes from a new server. How forwarding interacts with DMARC and what a small business can do.

S

SPF Softfail vs Hardfail: What Is the Difference?
SPF softfail (~all) is a weak warning and hardfail (-all) says unauthorized servers are not allowed. What the difference means for your domain.

W

What Are DMARC rua Reports?
rua reports are DMARC aggregate reports that mail providers send showing who uses your domain to send email. What they mean for a small business.
What Are DMARC ruf Reports?
ruf reports are DMARC failure reports about individual messages that failed checks. What they are and whether a small business needs them.
What Does DMARC p=none Mean?
p=none is the DMARC monitoring setting that asks receivers to take no action on failing mail. What it means and why it is not enough alone.
What Does DMARC p=quarantine Mean?
p=quarantine asks receivers to treat mail that fails DMARC as suspicious, often sending it to spam. What it means for a small business.
What Does DMARC p=reject Mean?
p=reject is the strictest DMARC setting, asking receivers to refuse mail that fails checks. What it means and what can go wrong if senders are missing.
What Is a Bulk Sender?
A bulk sender sends large volumes of email, such as newsletters or notices. Why mail providers expect authentication and what small businesses should do.
What Is a DKIM Selector?
A DKIM selector is the name that identifies which DKIM key to use for a message, so one domain can have several. What it means in practice.
What Is a DMARC Policy?
A DMARC policy is the p= setting that tells receivers to take no action, quarantine or reject mail that fails checks. Meaning and what to do.
What Is a DMARC Report Service?
A DMARC report service collects and turns raw DMARC reports into readable summaries of who sends email as your domain. When it helps.
What Is a DMARC Subdomain Policy?
The DMARC subdomain policy (sp tag) sets how receivers treat mail from subdomains of your domain. What it means and what to do.
What Is a DNS Host?
A DNS host is the service that holds and serves your domain's DNS records. How to find yours and why it matters for email security changes.
What Is a Domain Registrar?
A registrar is the company where a domain name is registered and renewed. How it relates to your DNS host and why it matters for email.
What Is a Lookalike Domain?
A lookalike domain is a different web address that closely resembles yours. Why DMARC cannot stop it and what a small business can do instead.
What Is a Mail Provider?
A mail provider is the service that hosts your business mailboxes and sends your email. Why it matters when setting up email protection.
What Is a Third-Party Sender?
A third-party sender is an outside service that sends email as your domain, such as billing or marketing tools. Why it matters for DMARC.
What Is a TXT Record?
A TXT record is a DNS entry that holds text, used to publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC settings. What it means for a small business.
What Is Account Takeover?
Account takeover is when someone else gains access to a real email account. Why DMARC does not prevent it and how small businesses reduce the risk.
What Is an Email Gateway?
An email gateway filters incoming and sometimes outgoing mail for spam and threats. How it differs from DMARC and whether a small business needs one.
What Is an Email Header?
An email header is the hidden technical information attached to every message, showing its path and authentication results. How to use it.
What Is an MX Record?
An MX record is a DNS entry that says which servers receive email for your domain. What it does and does not do for email security.
What Is BIMI?
BIMI lets a business display its logo next to authenticated email in supporting inboxes. How it relates to DMARC and whether you need it.
What Is Business Email Compromise (BEC)?
Business email compromise (BEC) is fraud where someone poses as a trusted contact by email to redirect money or data. Meaning and what to do.
What Is Display-Name Spoofing?
Display-name spoofing shows a trusted person's name on an email sent from an unrelated address. Why DMARC cannot stop it and what to do.
What Is DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)?
DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a digital signature to outgoing email, checked via a public key in DNS. Meaning for a small business.
What Is DMARC Alignment?
Alignment means the domain verified by SPF or DKIM matches the From address people see. Why DMARC needs it and what a small business should do.
What Is DMARC Enforcement?
DMARC enforcement means your policy is quarantine or reject, so receivers act on failing mail. What it means and how to reach it safely.
What Is DMARC?
DMARC is a DNS record that tells receivers what to do with email that fails SPF and DKIM checks for your domain. Meaning for a small business.
What Is DNS?
DNS is the internet's address book that connects domain names to services such as websites and email. What it means for a small business.
What Is Email Authentication?
Email authentication is the set of checks, SPF, DKIM and DMARC, that help receivers verify who really sent a message. Meaning for small businesses.
What Is Email Deliverability?
Deliverability is how likely your email is to reach the inbox instead of spam or being rejected. How authentication and DMARC relate to it.
What Is Email Spoofing?
Email spoofing is faking the sender of a message so it appears to come from someone else. What it means for a small business and what to do.
What Is MTA-STS?
MTA-STS tells other mail servers to use encrypted connections when delivering mail to your domain. How it differs from DMARC.
What Is Phishing?
Phishing is a message that tricks someone into giving up money, passwords or information. What it means for a small business and what to do.
What Is Spear Phishing?
Spear phishing is a targeted phishing message tailored to a specific person or company. What it means for a small business and what to do.
What Is SPF (Sender Policy Framework)?
SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a DNS record listing which servers may send email for your domain. What it means for a small business.
What Is the DMARC pct Tag?
The DMARC pct tag applies your policy to only a share of failing mail, so you can phase in enforcement. What it means and how to use it.
What Is the SPF Lookup Limit?
SPF allows at most 10 DNS lookups per check. Why exceeding it breaks SPF and what a small business should do about it.
