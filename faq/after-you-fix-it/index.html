After You Fix It: What Happens Next, FAQ

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026

Once you have followed the Fix it steps, your domain publishes a DMARC policy of p=reject. That asks receiving mail systems to refuse messages that fail authentication for your domain, which makes it much harder for strangers to send mail that looks like it came from you.

The setting stays in place, but your email world can change. A new billing tool, a marketing service or a new mail provider can start sending as your domain, and each needs to be set up properly. This page covers what to watch for, how to undo a change in one step, and what the optional quarterly re-test adds.

Questions and answers

What happens after I finish the Fix it steps?

Your domain publishes a DMARC policy of p=reject, which asks receiving mail systems to refuse messages that fail authentication for it. From then on, the main job is to make sure any new service that sends email as you is set up properly.

Is the work done forever once I finish?

Not quite. The setting stays in place until someone changes it, but your email setup can change. Adding a new billing, newsletter or CRM service, or switching mail providers, may require updating your records so legitimate mail keeps passing. A quick test message after any change is a good habit.

What should I do when I add a new service that sends email as my company?

Before you start sending, set up that service's authentication for your domain, usually a DKIM record and sometimes an SPF change, following the service's instructions. Then send a test message. Skipping this can cause its mail to be rejected under p=reject. Ask the service for its email authentication or custom domain instructions.

How will I know if legitimate email is being rejected?

Usually someone tells you that your messages did not arrive or bounced. Bounce messages and DMARC reports can also show failures. If it happens, check which service sent the mail, and fix its setup, or temporarily roll back the policy in one step.

Can I go back to a weaker setting?

Yes. Rollback is one step: you change the policy back at your DNS host. This is the safety valve if a legitimate sender is blocked. Fix the sender, then restore the stricter policy so you are protected again. Allow a little time for the change to spread before you test.

What is the quarterly re-test?

The re-test is an optional add-on at $99 a year, billed yearly. We re-check your public DNS every quarter and tell you if your setup has changed. It renews each year until you cancel.

Do I need the quarterly re-test?

No. It is optional, and Fix it works without it. They suit owners who prefer a later check rather than relying on memory. If your email setup rarely changes and you are comfortable checking, you can skip both without losing anything you already paid for.

Does p=reject protect me completely?

No. It makes it much harder to send mail that looks like it is from your exact domain, but it does not stop lookalike domains, display-name tricks or hacked mailboxes. Keep strong passwords, multi-factor sign-in and staff awareness in place, and confirm any payment or bank-detail change by phone.

Will my emails still reach customers?

Yes, as long as your legitimate senders are properly authenticated. Following the checks in the instructions confirms that before enforcement. Delivery also depends on other factors, such as message content and sender reputation, that DMARC does not control, so a test message to a few outside addresses is a good habit.

Should I tell my staff or vendors about the change?

Usually no announcement is needed, since nothing changes in how you send. It is worth telling whoever manages your website, billing or marketing tools that your domain now enforces DMARC, so they set up authentication before adding new senders. A short note to them is usually all it takes.

What if I change my domain host or mail provider?

Moving DNS hosts or mail providers means your records must be recreated or updated. Copy your DMARC, SPF and DKIM records carefully, and test afterwards. The optional quarterly re-test can confirm nothing was lost in the move. Do the move at a quiet time, and keep a copy of your old records.

How do I get help later?

Contact PlainArmor support and describe what you are seeing. Support replies within one business day. We cannot access your DNS, but we can help you work out which record or sender is likely the cause and what to change next.
