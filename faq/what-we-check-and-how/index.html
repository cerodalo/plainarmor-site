What PlainArmor Checks and How: FAQ

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026

PlainArmor's check is deliberately simple. We look up the public DNS records for your company's domain, the same records every receiving mail server reads, and see whether they tell the world to reject email that falsely claims to come from you.

If the records leave a gap, we draw a mock fake email on your screen so you can see what a stranger could attempt. It is never sent. We do not log in anywhere, read your mail, or change a setting. The answers below explain what we look at, how to read the result, and the limits of a check based only on public information.

Questions and answers

What does PlainArmor check?

PlainArmor checks your domain's public DNS records for email authentication settings, mainly DMARC, and looks at related SPF and DKIM information. The goal is to see whether anything tells receiving mail systems to reject a message that pretends to come from your domain. We read only what is already public.

How does the check work?

The check looks up your domain's public DNS records, the same lookups any mail server performs when a message arrives. We read the DMARC record and related records, then judge whether a stranger could send mail that looks like it is from you. No login, installation or access to your systems is needed.

What is the mock fake email?

The mock fake email is an example message drawn on your screen showing what a stranger could try if your domain is not protected. It is never sent to anyone. It exists only so you can see the risk in concrete terms instead of reading a technical report.

Is the fake email actually sent to my inbox or my staff?

No. PlainArmor never sends the fake email to your inbox, your staff, your customers or anyone else. It is a picture on a web page. You will not find it in any mailbox, and nobody on your team needs to be warned about it.

What result means my domain has a gap?

A gap usually means your domain has no DMARC record, or has one set to p=none, which only asks receivers to monitor and not to act. In either case, nothing instructs mail systems to reject fake messages that use your domain in the From address.

What if my check says I am already protected?

If your DMARC setting already instructs receivers to reject or quarantine failing mail, the check will reflect that and you do not need to buy anything to close a gap. Protection also depends on your legitimate senders being set up correctly, which public records alone cannot fully show.

Does the check look at my email content or contacts?

No. PlainArmor does not read your email, contacts, files or accounts. The check works only from public DNS records about your domain. Message content lives in your mailboxes, which we never access and have no way of seeing. This is also why we never need a password from you.

Why does the check focus on DMARC?

DMARC is the setting that tells receiving mail systems what to do with messages that fail authentication for your domain. Without a firm DMARC policy, receivers decide for themselves, and fakes using your exact domain may be delivered. It is the key control for this problem, and the one our instructions focus on.

How long does the check take?

The check itself is quick because it only reads public DNS records. Results are shown on screen once the lookup finishes. There is nothing to install and nothing for you to prepare beforehand beyond knowing your company's domain name. The steps you follow afterward take longer, because they include waiting periods for safety.

Can the check be wrong?

The check reads public records accurately, but it cannot see everything. It cannot know every service that legitimately sends email for you, or what a receiving provider does internally. Treat it as a clear snapshot of your published settings, not a full security audit.

Does PlainArmor check lookalike domains?

No. The check covers your own domain only. A lookalike domain, such as one with a swapped letter, belongs to someone else and DMARC on your domain cannot control it. Our guide on what PlainArmor does not protect against explains what else can help.

Do I need to own the domain to run a check?

Because the check reads only public records, anyone can look up any domain. You should run it on a domain that your business owns or manages, since only the domain owner or their DNS host can make the changes the instructions describe.
