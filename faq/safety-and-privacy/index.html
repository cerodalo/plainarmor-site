PlainArmor Safety and Privacy: FAQ

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026

Safety is the core design choice at PlainArmor. We read only public DNS records, the same information any mail server looks up. We never send an email, real or fake. We never log in to your accounts, and we never touch your DNS or any other setting.

The fake email you see during the check is a mock drawn on screen. It is not delivered anywhere. Fix it is a set of instructions you follow yourself, so you stay in control of every change. The answers below explain what we access, what we do not, and how to undo a change if you need to.

Questions and answers

Is PlainArmor safe to use?

Yes, because of how it is built: PlainArmor reads only public DNS records, sends nothing to anyone, and never touches your settings. It needs no password, no login and no access to your accounts. The worst a check can do is tell you something you did not know.

What information does PlainArmor look at?

PlainArmor looks only at public DNS records for your domain, information anyone on the internet can look up. It does not see your email content, contacts, files, passwords or private settings. Nothing we read requires your permission or any kind of access.

Does PlainArmor send any emails to test my domain?

No. PlainArmor never sends fake or test emails. The mock fake email you see is drawn on screen only and is never delivered to any inbox. No message leaves our system pretending to be you, so there is no risk of confusing staff or customers.

Will PlainArmor change my DNS or email settings?

No. We never touch your DNS or any other setting. Fix it gives you instructions, and you make each change yourself, or hand the steps to whoever manages your domain. You decide when and whether to apply each step, and you can pause at any point without penalty.

Do I need to give PlainArmor my passwords or account access?

No. PlainArmor never asks for your registrar, DNS host or email passwords. If anyone claiming to be PlainArmor asks for them, treat it as suspicious and contact us through our site. You only log in to your own accounts, yourself, and nobody else needs your credentials to follow the steps.

What can go wrong if I follow the instructions?

The main risk is that a strict setting can block legitimate email if one of your real senders, such as a billing or newsletter service, is not set up. That is why the instructions check that mail still works first, and why undoing a change is a single step.

Why does the staged route exist?

The staged route exists for businesses where other services send email as their domain. It adds checkpoints at days 7, 14, 21 and 28 and always checks that your mail still works first, so a strict setting does not accidentally block a real sender.

Can I undo a change if something breaks?

Yes. Rollback is one step: you restore the earlier setting at your DNS host. The instructions tell you exactly which value to put back. Because DNS changes can take a little time to spread, you may need to wait briefly for the undo to take full effect.

Does PlainArmor store my business information?

PlainArmor handles the details needed to run the check and fulfil your purchase, such as your domain and contact details. We do not access your mailboxes or accounts. For specifics on what is kept and for how long, see the privacy information on the PlainArmor site. We do not sell your mailbox data, because we never have it.

Could running a check on my domain alert attackers?

No. The check reads public records, the same lookups mail servers do all day, and it does not signal anything to third parties. Your domain's DNS records are already visible to anyone who looks, so reading them reveals nothing new, and nothing about the lookup exposes your mail, accounts or settings.

Does PlainArmor make security guarantees?

No. We do not guarantee that you will never be impersonated or that no email will ever be misdelivered. A strong DMARC setting greatly narrows one specific problem, but it has limits, which we describe openly in our promise and limits guides.

Is this legal or compliance advice?

No. PlainArmor provides practical technical instructions, not legal advice. If you need to understand legal or regulatory obligations for your business, speak with a qualified professional. Our instructions focus on the technical steps for your domain's email settings, and PlainArmor is available to US businesses only.
