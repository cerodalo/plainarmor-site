Email Spoofing Basics: Plain-English FAQ

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026

Email spoofing means sending a message that falsely looks like it came from someone else. Because the From address in email is just text the sender fills in, a stranger can type your company's domain there. Unless your domain publishes a firm DMARC policy, nothing tells receiving systems to reject it.

Spoofing is not the same as your account being hacked, and it is not the only way to impersonate a business. Lookalike domains and display-name tricks are separate. This page explains the basics in plain English, what spoofing can lead to, and which defenses address which problem. Nothing here is meant to alarm you; spoofing is a common, well-understood weakness with practical remedies.

Questions and answers

What is email spoofing?

Email spoofing is sending a message that falsely appears to come from someone else, such as your company's domain. The sender simply types your address in the From field. Email was designed without a built-in check on that field, which is why add-on authentication settings exist.

Can someone really send an email that looks like it came from my company?

Yes, if your domain has no strong DMARC setting. Without it, nothing tells receiving mail systems to reject messages that use your exact domain in the From address. Some receivers may filter them anyway, but you are relying on their guesswork.

Why is email so easy to fake?

The original email system trusted the sender to state who they were. The From address is just text the sender fills in. SPF, DKIM and DMARC were added later to let domain owners publish rules, but a rule only works if you publish it.

Why would someone fake my company's email?

Attackers use a trusted name to get a reply or a click. A message that appears to come from a real vendor, owner or business is more likely to be believed. Typical goals include getting a payment sent, a login entered or a file opened.

Does spoofing mean my email account was hacked?

No. Spoofing means someone is imitating your address from outside; your mailbox is not involved and your password is not exposed. A hacked mailbox is a different problem, called account takeover, and needs different defenses such as strong passwords and multi-factor sign-in.

Who is affected when my domain is spoofed?

Usually the people who receive the fake: your customers, vendors or staff. They may be misled by a message that appears to come from you. You may only find out when one of them asks whether a strange message was really yours.

What is the difference between spoofing and phishing?

Spoofing is the faking of a sender's identity. Phishing is the goal: tricking someone into giving up money, passwords or information. Phishing messages often use spoofing, but they can also come from lookalike domains or hacked accounts, so stopping one does not stop the others.

What is a lookalike domain?

A lookalike domain is a different domain that resembles yours, such as one with a swapped or added letter. Because it is a separate domain owned by someone else, DMARC on your domain cannot block it. Awareness, staff habits and monitoring can help.

Does DMARC stop all fake email?

No. DMARC stops messages that use your exact domain in the From address from passing as genuine when you enforce it. It does not stop lookalike domains, display-name tricks or mail sent from a hacked mailbox. It is one important layer, not a complete shield.

What is display-name spoofing?

Display-name spoofing is when the visible sender name says your company or owner's name, but the actual address is from an unrelated domain. DMARC on your domain cannot stop it, since your domain is not used. Checking the full address before acting is the main defense.

How can I tell whether my domain can be spoofed?

Check whether your domain has a DMARC record and whether its policy is quarantine or reject. No record, or p=none, usually means nothing instructs receivers to act on fakes. Should I warn my customers about spoofing?

A short, calm note is reasonable if you have reason to worry, such as a fake reaching a customer. Tell them how you really communicate, for example that you never change bank details by email. Avoid alarming language, and invite them to call you if a message ever seems off. Fixing your DMARC setting also helps going forward.
