How to read your DMARC record

TL;DR: A DMARC record is one line of tag=value pairs separated by semicolons. The two that matter most are p (what receivers should do with failing mail) and rua (where reports go). Everything else tunes the details.

The record is a list

Here is a typical record. It lives as a TXT record at _dmarc.example.com:

v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=100; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com; adkim=r; aspf=r

Read it left to right as a list of settings. Each is name=value, and a semicolon ends it.

Tag by tag

v=DMARC1

The version. It must come first. If it is missing or misspelled, receivers ignore the record.

p=

The policy for your domain. Three values, defined in RFC 7489:

  • none: no specific action requested. Monitoring only.
  • quarantine: treat failing mail as suspicious, usually the spam folder.
  • reject: refuse failing mail during delivery.

p is the only required tag besides v. See our guide on the three policies.

rua=

Where to send aggregate reports, as mailto: addresses. These are daily summaries from receivers listing which servers sent mail as your domain and whether they passed. They are the best way to find forgotten senders. Use an address someone checks, or a monitoring service.

Several addresses can be separated by commas:

rua=mailto:a@example.com,mailto:b@example.com

ruf=

Where to send failure (forensic) reports for individual messages. Few receivers send them, so do not rely on it.

pct=

The percentage of failing messages the policy applies to. The default is 100. Setting pct=25 with p=quarantine applies the policy to a portion of failing mail, and the remainder is handled as if the policy were one step milder. Some use it for a gradual rollout. Receiver behavior can vary, so treat it as a soft control.

sp=

The policy for subdomains such as news.example.com. If it is missing, subdomains inherit p. If you have a subdomain that sends mail you have not reviewed, check this one.

adkim= and aspf=

The alignment mode for DKIM and SPF. Values are r (relaxed) and s (strict). Both default to relaxed. Relaxed accepts a related subdomain; strict requires an exact domain match. Leave these alone unless you know why. Our alignment guide explains the idea.

fo=

Controls when failure reports are requested. Rarely needed.

Four sample records, decoded

Monitoring:

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

Plain English: "Do nothing to failing mail yet. Send me summaries."

Partial enforcement:

v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=25; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

Plain English: "For a quarter of failing mail, send it to spam. Send me summaries."

Full quarantine:

v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

Reject with a stricter subdomain policy:

v=DMARC1; p=reject; sp=reject; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

Plain English: "Refuse failing mail for this domain and every subdomain. Send me summaries."

Common mistakes

  • Two records. Only one DMARC record per name.
  • Missing semicolons. Tags must be separated by ;.
  • Quote marks and spaces. Some DNS hosts add quotes automatically; do not add them twice.
  • Wrong name. The host must be _dmarc, with the underscore. Some DNS hosts add your domain automatically, so entering _dmarc.example.com could produce _dmarc.example.com.example.com.
  • Report address nobody reads. Reports are the point of p=none.
  • Report address on another domain. Reports to an outside domain require that domain to publish permission under RFC 7489. Use a mailbox on your own domain to keep it simple.

What to do with what you read

  • If you see p=none, plan the move to enforcement after you have reviewed reports.
  • If there is no rua, add one so you learn which senders exist.
  • If you see p=reject, check that real mail is passing.

A hypothetical read-through

A landscaping office at greenfield.example has v=DMARC1; p=none. There is no rua, so it has had a policy for years but has never received a report. It cannot tell if any fake mail is out there or whether its own invoicing tool authenticates. The fix is one line: add rua=mailto:.... This example is invented.

Reading someone else's record

A lookup of other companies' records is a good way to practice, since DNS is public. You will notice that many large organizations publish p=reject with several report addresses, and some include sp= and fo= tags. You will also see plenty of p=none records. Seeing both is a reminder that there is no single right setting for everyone; there is a right setting for your stage.

When you read a record, finish with three checks:

  1. Does it start with v=DMARC1?
  2. Is p set to what you intended?
  3. Does rua point to a mailbox or service that someone actually reads?

If you can answer yes to all three, you understand your record better than most.

Key takeaways

  • A DMARC record is tag=value pairs separated by semicolons at _dmarc.yourdomain.
  • p is the action; rua is the report address.
  • pct and sp fine-tune rollout and subdomains.
  • Publish only one record, and keep the syntax clean.
  • Reports are how you find forgotten senders.

Frequently asked questions

Where is my DMARC record?

It is a DNS TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com, for example _dmarc.example.com.

Do I need ruf?

No. ruf requests detailed failure reports, and many receivers do not send them because of privacy concerns. rua aggregate reports are the useful ones.

What happens if the record has a typo?

Receivers may ignore it, treating your domain as having no DMARC policy. Check that it starts with v=DMARC1 and uses semicolons between tags.

Can I have two DMARC records?

No. Publish one. Multiple records can cause receivers to ignore the policy.
