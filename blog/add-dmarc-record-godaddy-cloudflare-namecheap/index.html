How to add a DMARC record at GoDaddy, Cloudflare, Namecheap, or any DNS host

TL;DR: At any DNS host you add one TXT record named _dmarc with a value such as v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourdomain.com. Menu names differ between providers, and they change, so we describe the fields rather than click paths. Always confirm with a lookup afterward.

A note on provider screens

We have not reproduced click-by-click steps for specific providers because their dashboards change often, and we want you to follow your provider's current help pages. What does not change is the record itself. If you search your provider's help for "add a TXT record," you will find the right page for your screen.

What you need to enter

Four fields, in nearly every DNS dashboard:

FieldEnter
TypeTXT
Name / Host_dmarc
Value / Contentyour DMARC text (below)
TTLleave at automatic, or 1 hour

The value to start with

Start in monitoring mode so nothing is blocked yet. Use a mailbox on your own domain for reports:

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

Replace example.com with your domain and make sure someone reads that mailbox (or use a monitoring service's address if you have one).

Step by step, at any host

  1. Find where your DNS is managed. It is wherever your nameservers point. If you bought a domain at one company but pointed nameservers to another service (for example, a content-delivery or website host), manage DNS there.
  2. Open the DNS records page for your domain.
  3. Check for an existing DMARC record. Look for a TXT record starting with _dmarc. If there is one, edit it instead of adding another. Two records break DMARC.
  4. Add a new record with the fields above.
  5. Save.
  6. Verify with a lookup.

Host-name quirks

The most common mistake is the Name field. Dashboards follow one of two styles:

  • Short style: you type _dmarc and the host appends your domain.
  • Full style: you must type _dmarc.example.com.

Look at your other records. If your existing records show names like @ or www rather than full domain names, use the short style. If the saved record shows _dmarc.example.com.example.com, you used the full name in a short-style field. Fix it.

Quotes and spaces

Some hosts add quote marks around TXT values for you. If you paste quotes too, the record may break. Paste the text without quotes first and see what the dashboard shows.

Long values sometimes get split into pieces. That is normal for TXT records and receivers rejoin them.

Verify it

After saving, check from a command line (replace the domain):

nslookup -type=TXT _dmarc.example.com

You should see your text. Or use any free DMARC lookup website, which also flags syntax errors. If it shows nothing, wait and try again, because caches can hold the old answer for a while.

What happens next

A p=none record does not change how mail is delivered. It asks receivers to send you reports. Over the next days you will start receiving them. Do not stop here: p=none does not ask receivers to block fakes. See our article on what p=none means.

The sequence is:

  1. Publish p=none with reporting.
  2. Review reports and fix legitimate senders.
  3. Move to p=quarantine, ideally gradually.
  4. Move to p=reject when reports are clean.

Our guide on moving from monitoring to enforcement covers steps 2 to 4.

Subdomains

By default subdomains inherit the main domain's policy. If you send mail from a subdomain, such as news.example.com, include it in your sender review. You can publish a separate record at _dmarc.news.example.com if needed.

Troubleshooting

  • Lookup shows nothing: the Name field is probably wrong, or the DNS is managed elsewhere.
  • Two records: delete one.
  • Typos in v=DMARC1: receivers may ignore the record.
  • No reports arrive: reports can take a day or more, and not every receiver sends them.
  • Reports go to the wrong place: check the rua spelling.

A hypothetical walk-through

An office manager at copperridge.example bought the domain years ago at one registrar and later pointed its nameservers at a website builder. She opens the registrar's DNS page and finds it empty, so she cannot find her existing records. The records live at the website builder, where she adds _dmarc and verifies with a lookup. This scenario is invented, but "wrong place" is a frequent snag.

Before you hit save: a short pre-flight list

A DNS change is easy to make and easy to forget, so take two minutes first.

  • Take a screenshot or copy of your current records. If something goes wrong, you can put things back.
  • Check who else uses this DNS. Your website, your mail and other services all depend on the same page. Change only the DMARC record.
  • Do not delete records you do not recognize. They may belong to a tool you forgot about, and removing them could stop that tool's mail or website features.
  • Tell your IT contact or web person that you are adding the record, so nobody undoes it by accident.
  • Write the date and the reason in a shared note. Six months from now, someone will wonder why _dmarc exists.

If you are unsure which DNS host is in charge, ask your registrar's support or whoever built your website. It is a normal question and they can usually answer it in a minute.

Key takeaways

  • The record is TXT, Name _dmarc, and a value starting v=DMARC1.
  • Start with p=none and a rua address.
  • Edit an existing record rather than adding a second.
  • Watch the Name field's quirks and quote marks.
  • Verify with a lookup, then proceed to enforcement in stages.

Frequently asked questions

Which DNS host do I use?

It is wherever your domain's nameservers point, which may differ from where you bought the domain. A DNS lookup of your NS records, or your registrar's dashboard, shows it.

Should the host be _dmarc or _dmarc.example.com?

Many hosts add your domain automatically, so enter just _dmarc. Others expect the full name. Check how the finished record displays and test with a lookup.

How long until it works?

DNS changes often appear within minutes but can take longer depending on the record's TTL and caching. Allow up to a day before worrying.

Do I need to keep my SPF and DKIM too?

Yes. DMARC relies on SPF and DKIM. It adds the policy and reporting on top; it does not replace them.
