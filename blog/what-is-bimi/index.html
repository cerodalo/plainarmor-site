What is BIMI, and does a small business need it?

TL;DR: BIMI is a standard that lets participating inboxes display your verified logo beside your messages. It needs DMARC at quarantine or reject, an SVG logo, and a paid certificate. It is a branding extra, not a security essential, so most small businesses should enforce DMARC first and treat BIMI as optional.

What BIMI is

BIMI stands for Brand Indicators for Message Identification. Google describes it as a standard that lets organizations display their logo in recipients' mailboxes, with the logo verified by a third party so recipients can be confident it is legitimate.

Think of it as a verified badge. Where supported, it replaces a generic initial or silhouette with your actual logo.

What it takes

Based on Google's current documentation for Gmail:

  1. DMARC enforcement. The policy must be quarantine or reject, not none, and the percent value must be 100.
  2. A certificate. Either a Verified Mark Certificate (VMC), which requires a registered trademark, or a Common Mark Certificate (CMC), which Google lists as an option for logos without a trademark. Both come from third-party certificate authorities and typically cost money.
  3. A logo in SVG format, meeting size rules.
  4. A BIMI DNS record pointing to the logo, hosted on a secure web server.

Other mailbox providers have their own rules, and support varies. Check each provider's documentation if a particular one matters to you.

What a BIMI record looks like

It is a TXT record at default._bimi.yourdomain. A placeholder example:

default._bimi.example.com   TXT   "v=BIMI1; l=https://example.com/logo.svg; a=https://example.com/cert.pem"

The l= points to the SVG logo and a= to the certificate. Your certificate authority will tell you exactly what to publish.

What BIMI does for you

  • Makes your messages more recognizable in supporting inboxes.
  • Reinforces your brand with customers who see your emails often.
  • Signals that you have enforcement-level DMARC in place, since it is required.

We will not claim measured gains in opens or trust. Those claims vary and we have not verified a source for them.

What BIMI does not do

  • It does not stop anyone from faking your domain. DMARC enforcement does that, and you need it first.
  • It does not cover lookalike domains.
  • It does not appear in every inbox.
  • It does not replace training or payment-verification habits.

Does a small business need it?

For most, no. Here is a simple test.

Skip it for now if:

  • You are not yet at quarantine or reject.
  • You send mostly one-to-one business email.
  • A certificate fee and logo work are not a priority.

Consider it if:

  • You already enforce DMARC and it is stable.
  • You send consumer-facing mail, such as receipts or reminders, where brand recognition matters.
  • You have a finished vector logo and, for a VMC, a registered trademark.

The order to do things

  1. SPF and DKIM for every sender.
  2. DMARC at p=none, reports, fixes.
  3. quarantine at 100 percent, then reject.
  4. Only then, BIMI, if the brand value justifies it.

A hypothetical example

A regional bakery at sunrisebakes.example sends order confirmations to many customers. It reaches p=reject at 100 percent after a few months and has a registered trademark. It decides BIMI is worth it and applies for a certificate. A neighboring accounting office that only emails clients one-on-one skips BIMI and uses its time on reports instead. Both choices are reasonable. This scenario is invented.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with BIMI before DMARC is stable.
  • Assuming a logo will appear everywhere.
  • Using a logo file that does not meet specs.
  • Letting the certificate expire.

What it costs in effort, not just money

Even before certificates, BIMI means some real work. You need a logo drawn as a clean vector file (SVG), which many small businesses do not have, because logos are often delivered as images. You need to host that file on a secure web address. You need DMARC at full enforcement. And you need to renew the certificate when it expires, or the logo stops showing.

For a company that is still sorting out its sender list, each of those is a distraction from the work that actually reduces fraud risk. That is why we suggest finishing DMARC first and revisiting BIMI once things are stable, if brand display matters to you at all.

Questions to ask before paying for a certificate

  • Which mailbox providers do my customers actually use, and do they show BIMI logos?
  • Do I have a registered trademark, or would a Common Mark Certificate fit?
  • Who will renew the certificate, and when does it expire?
  • Is my DMARC policy stable at quarantine or reject at 100 percent?

If you cannot answer these comfortably, wait.

Key takeaways

  • BIMI shows a verified logo in supporting inboxes.
  • Gmail requires DMARC at quarantine or reject at 100 percent, an SVG logo, and a VMC or CMC.
  • It is branding, not security by itself.
  • Enforce DMARC first; BIMI is optional for most small businesses.
  • Support differs by mailbox provider.

Frequently asked questions

Does BIMI stop spoofing?

No. BIMI is a display feature. Its DMARC requirement, however, means you must already enforce a policy, and that part does help against exact-domain spoofing.

Does Gmail show my logo automatically if I enforce DMARC?

No. Per Google's documentation, Gmail's BIMI support also requires a third-party certificate and an SVG logo, besides DMARC enforcement.

Do I need a trademark?

For a Verified Mark Certificate, Google's documentation says trademark registration is required. A Common Mark Certificate is an alternative for logos without a trademark.

Should I do BIMI before DMARC?

No. DMARC enforcement is a prerequisite. Do that first.
