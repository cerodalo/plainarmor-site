What to do when DMARC breaks your invoices or newsletters

By The PlainArmor Team · Last reviewed October 3, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR: If legitimate mail started failing after a DMARC change, loosen the policy one step to stop the damage, identify which sender is failing, set up DKIM (or SPF) for it with your own domain, and then tighten again. The cause is almost always a service that sends as you but was never authenticated.

First: stop the bleeding

If customers are not receiving invoices right now:

  1. Loosen the policy one step. Change p=reject to p=quarantine, or p=quarantine to p=none. Keep the rua tag so reports continue.
  2. Tell the people affected if it was something important, such as invoices or receipts. A short note and a resend is fine.
  3. Do not delete the record unless you have no choice; you want the reports.

Policy changes may take time to reach receivers because of DNS caching, so do this early.

Second: find which sender failed

Clues come from three places.

Bounce messages. A rejected message often includes text saying it failed DMARC or the sending domain's policy. Note the sending service named in the bounce.

Message headers. In a delivered or quarantined test, look for:

dmarc=fail (p=quarantine) header.from=example.com
spf=pass smtp.mailfrom=bounce.vendor.example
dkim=none

This example reads: SPF passed, but for the vendor's domain rather than yours. DKIM was missing. DMARC failed because nothing aligned with example.com.

Aggregate reports. Find the sending source with failing rows and match it to a service by IP address owner. See our guide on finding your senders.

Third: understand why it failed

The most common cause is alignment. DMARC does not just ask "did SPF or DKIM pass?" It asks whether the domain that passed matches your From domain. A newsletter service may send from its own servers using its own bounce domain. SPF passes for them, not for you. Without DKIM signing in your name, DMARC fails.

Other causes:

CauseWhat happened
No DKIM at the vendorMail is not signed with your domain
SPF include missingVendor's servers are not authorized for your domain
Subdomain mismatchVendor uses a subdomain and your sp= or alignment mode is stricter than expected
ForwardingSPF broke on forward, and no DKIM to fall back on
Mailing listThe list altered the message and broke the DKIM signature
Old recordVendor changed servers; your SPF is outdated

Fourth: fix the sender

Search the vendor's help for "authenticate your domain," "sender authentication," or "custom DKIM." You are looking for two things:

  • A DKIM record or two to publish in your DNS, which makes the vendor sign mail with your domain.
  • Sometimes an SPF include or a custom return-path domain.

Publish what they give you, wait for their tool to confirm, and send a test message. In the headers you want dkim=pass with your domain, and dmarc=pass.

Do this for every failing sender, not just the one that was noticed first.

Fifth: re-tighten, slowly

Once reports are clean:

  • Use pct to ease in again.
  • Wait a full cycle (such as a billing month) before returning to reject.
  • Write down your sender list so it is not lost.

Our guide on moving from monitoring to enforcement walks through the stages.

A hypothetical example

A small online shop at bluefinch.example moves to p=reject. A week later, customers say they never received order confirmations. The owner loosens the policy to p=quarantine, then checks a bounce: the order-confirmation tool sends from the vendor's servers with no DKIM for bluefinch.example. She publishes the DKIM records the vendor provides, sees dkim=pass on a test, waits two weeks, and tightens again. This is an invented scenario.

Prevention checklist

  • Inventory senders before enforcing.
  • Observe at p=none through a full billing and newsletter cycle.
  • Use pct to ramp.
  • Send test messages from each tool after any change.
  • Have one person own the sender list.

When to get help

If you cannot identify the sender, if you do not control your DNS, or if mail is business-critical and failing, ask your IT contractor or the vendor's support. Share the headers from a failed message; they contain most of what is needed.

What to ask the vendor's support team

When you contact a vendor, a specific question gets a faster answer than "DMARC is broken." Try:

"Our domain is example.com. Mail sent through your service fails DMARC. We want it to be signed with DKIM using our own domain so it aligns. Can you send the steps to set up custom domain authentication, including the DNS records we need to publish?"

Have these ready:

  • Your domain name and the From address the tool uses.
  • A copy of the headers from a failed or quarantined message.
  • The name of the account owner at your company.

Some vendors charge for custom domain authentication on certain plans, and some use different words for it, such as "branded sending" or "dedicated domain." If a vendor cannot sign with your domain at all, consider sending that mail from a subdomain with its own DMARC record, or ask whether another tool better fits your needs.

Key takeaways

  • Loosen the policy one step first, keep reporting.
  • Find the failing sender from bounces, headers and reports.
  • Alignment is the usual culprit: the vendor authenticated its own domain, not yours.
  • Fix with DKIM at the vendor using your domain.
  • Re-tighten gradually.

Frequently asked questions

Should I remove my DMARC record?

Usually it is better to loosen the policy one step (for example reject to quarantine, or quarantine to none) than to delete the record, because you keep reporting while you fix things.

Why does a tool pass SPF but still fail DMARC?

DMARC needs alignment. The domain that SPF or DKIM authenticated must match your From domain. Many third-party tools authenticate their own domain unless you set up custom domain signing.

How do I find which sender broke?

Check the bounce message or spam headers for dmarc=fail, then match the sending source in your aggregate reports.

How soon after a DNS change will mail recover?

Often within hours, but cached records can delay it. Allow time and re-test with a message to an outside mailbox.
