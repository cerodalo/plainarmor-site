DMARC alignment explained

TL;DR: Alignment means the domain that SPF or DKIM verified is the same as, or a close relative of, the domain in the visible From address. DMARC passes only when SPF or DKIM passes and aligns. This is why a vendor's mail can "pass" yet still fail DMARC.

The problem alignment solves

Before DMARC, SPF and DKIM could pass for any domain at all. A scammer could send from their own domain, pass SPF for that domain, and show your address in the From line. Everything technically "passed," yet the message was a fake.

DMARC closes that gap by asking a further question: does the domain that passed match the domain the reader sees?

Two addresses in every email

An email has more than one sender address:

  • The From address is what you see: billing@example.com.
  • The envelope sender (also called the return path or bounce address) is hidden, used by servers during delivery.

SPF checks the envelope sender's domain. DKIM checks the domain named in the signature, written d= in the signature header. DMARC compares each to the From domain.

SPF alignment

SPF passes for the envelope domain. For SPF to align, that envelope domain must match the From domain.

Example:

  • From: billing@example.com
  • Envelope sender: bounce@mail.vendor.example
  • SPF passes for vendor.example servers.
  • The domains do not match example.com, so SPF is not aligned.

DKIM alignment

DKIM passes for the signing domain. For DKIM to align, the signing domain must match the From domain.

Example:

  • From: billing@example.com
  • Signature d=vendor.example: valid but not aligned.
  • Signature d=example.com: valid and aligned.

Relaxed vs strict

DMARC offers two matching modes, set with aspf= for SPF and adkim= for DKIM. Under RFC 7489 both default to relaxed (r).

ModeWhat counts as a matchExample
RelaxedSame organizational domainmail.example.com matches example.com
StrictExact domainmail.example.com does not match example.com

Leave these at their defaults unless you have a reason. Strict can make legitimate subdomain mail fail.

The pass rule

A message passes DMARC when at least one of these is true:

  1. SPF passes and is aligned.
  2. DKIM passes and is aligned.

It does not need both. That is why DKIM on your own domain matters: it can carry the message even when SPF breaks, for instance after forwarding.

Why third-party tools often fail

Many newsletter, billing and CRM tools send from their own infrastructure by default. They use their own envelope domain and sign with their own key. Both checks pass for the vendor's domain. Neither aligns with yours. DMARC fails.

The fix is almost always in the vendor's settings, under a name like "authenticate your domain" or "custom DKIM." Once they sign with a key for your domain (you publish a DNS record they give you), DKIM aligns.

Before:  From: news@example.com   DKIM d=vendor.example   -> not aligned
After:   From: news@example.com   DKIM d=example.com      -> aligned

A hypothetical example

A small yoga studio at stillwater.example sends class reminders through a booking tool. Its DMARC reports show the tool passing SPF and DKIM, yet failing DMARC. The tool authenticates bookingtool.example. The studio publishes the DKIM records the tool provides, so mail is signed as stillwater.example, and the next reports show an aligned pass. This is an invented scenario.

How to check alignment yourself

Send a test message from the tool to an outside mailbox and view the original headers. Look for:

dkim=pass header.d=example.com
spf=pass smtp.mailfrom=bounce.vendor.example
dmarc=pass header.from=example.com

Here DKIM aligns (header.d matches), SPF does not, and DMARC passes thanks to DKIM.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming "spf=pass" means safe. Check whose domain passed.
  • Setting strict modes without need.
  • Forgetting subdomain senders.
  • Looking only at pass/fail and not at the domains named.

Subdomains and alignment

Relaxed alignment treats a subdomain and its parent as the same organization. That is handy: if your newsletter tool signs as mail.example.com and your From address is news@example.com, relaxed alignment counts that as a match.

Things get more delicate in two cases.

Strict mode. If you set adkim=s or aspf=s, only an exact match counts. The same newsletter would fail.

Different domains entirely. If your From address uses example.com but a tool signs with example-mail.com, that is not the same organization, so it will not align in either mode.

Also remember that sp= in your DMARC record sets the policy for subdomains. A subdomain used for bulk mail can carry its own record at _dmarc.news.example.com if you want a different policy from your main domain. This is a common way to keep marketing mail from putting your main domain's invoices at risk while you tighten up.

When in doubt, send a test and read the headers. The three words to look for are dkim=pass, spf=pass and dmarc=pass, and the domain named beside each.

Key takeaways

  • DMARC needs SPF or DKIM to pass and align with the visible From domain.
  • SPF aligns on the hidden envelope domain; DKIM aligns on the signing domain.
  • Relaxed mode is the default and usually right.
  • Third-party tools usually need custom domain authentication to align.
  • Check headers, not just pass/fail labels.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between passing and aligned?

Passing means the check succeeded for some domain. Aligned means that domain matches the domain in the visible From address. DMARC requires an aligned pass from SPF or DKIM.

Should I use strict alignment?

Most small businesses leave the default, relaxed. Strict requires exact domain matches and raises the chance of breaking legitimate mail.

Why do newsletter tools fail DMARC?

Often they send with their own bounce domain and sign with their own DKIM key. Both pass, but for their domain, not yours. Setting up custom domain authentication fixes it.

Do I need both SPF and DKIM aligned?

No. RFC 7489 requires at least one to pass with alignment. Having both gives a safety net.
