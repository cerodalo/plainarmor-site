How to turn on DKIM in Google Workspace

TL;DR: In the Google Admin console, go to Authenticate email, generate a DKIM key for your domain, publish the TXT record it gives you at your DNS host, then return and click Start authentication. Per Google's help, it can take up to 48 hours to begin working.

What DKIM does

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) lets a sending system attach a digital signature to each message. The matching public key sits in your DNS. A receiver uses the key to check that the message was signed by your domain and was not changed on the way.

Unlike SPF, DKIM survives most forwarding, which is why DMARC works best when both are in place.

Before you start

  • You need Google Workspace administrator access.
  • You need access to your DNS host. Often that is not Google.
  • Menu names change. These steps follow Google's current help page, and you should compare with it when you go.

Step by step

1. Open the setting

In the Admin console, follow the path Google documents: Menu, then Apps, then Google Workspace, then Gmail, then Authenticate email.

2. Pick your domain and generate the key

Select your domain from the list and choose to generate a new record. Google offers two key lengths. Its documentation recommends 2048-bit if your DNS provider can handle it, and offers 1024-bit for providers with limited support for long records.

You can keep the default selector prefix, which is google.

3. Copy the two values

Google shows you:

  • DNS host name, typically google._domainkey.
  • TXT record value, a long string starting with v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p= followed by the key.

Copy both exactly.

4. Publish it at your DNS host

Add a TXT record:

FieldValue
TypeTXT
Namegoogle._domainkey (some hosts need the full name google._domainkey.example.com)
Valuethe long string from Google

Some DNS hosts have a character limit on one value and want a long key split into pieces. Check your host's help if you get an error. Do not edit the key text.

5. Start authentication

Return to the Authenticate email page, select your domain, and click Start authentication. If Google says the record is not found, wait and try again. Google's help notes the page may keep showing a message to update DNS for up to 48 hours.

Verify it

Send a test message from a Google Workspace address to a personal mailbox at another provider. Open the message's original headers. Look for something like:

dkim=pass header.d=example.com

The domain after header.d= should be your domain, not google.com. If it shows google.com, DKIM signing for your custom domain is not active yet.

Common problems

  • Typed the key by hand. Always copy and paste.
  • Name doubled up. If your host appends the domain, entering the full name creates google._domainkey.example.com.example.com.
  • Added to the wrong DNS. Your nameservers decide where DNS lives.
  • Quotes added twice.
  • Stopped at the DNS step. The key does nothing until you click Start authentication.
  • Other senders unsigned. This setting covers Google's mail only. Your invoicing and newsletter tools need their own DKIM setup.

A hypothetical example

A three-person bookkeeping firm at quillandledger.example has used Google Workspace for years and never turned on DKIM. An administrator generates a 2048-bit key, adds the TXT record at the firm's DNS host, waits a day, and clicks Start authentication. A test message to a personal inbox shows dkim=pass for the firm's domain. This is an invented scenario.

Why it matters for DMARC

DMARC passes when at least one of SPF or DKIM passes and aligns with the From domain. DKIM signed with your own domain aligns cleanly. Having it means that forwarding or SPF problems will not make your legitimate mail fail. See our guide on alignment.

What DKIM does not do

A DKIM signature proves a message was signed by a domain. It does not stop someone from sending unsigned mail that fakes your From line. Only a DMARC policy at quarantine or reject asks receivers to act on that. DKIM is a building block, not a shield.

Key rotation

Keys can be replaced over time. Google's help describes generating a new record. If you rotate, publish the new record before activating it, and avoid leaving the domain without a working key.

If you have more than one domain

Google Workspace lets you add secondary domains and domain aliases. DKIM is set up per domain, so if you send mail as more than one domain, repeat the process for each. On the Authenticate email page, use the domain dropdown to select each one in turn.

Do not forget domains that only send occasionally, such as a second brand or an old name. If a domain does not send mail at all, there is nothing to sign; publish a "no mail" SPF and a DMARC record instead.

Key takeaways

  • Path in Admin console: Apps, Google Workspace, Gmail, Authenticate email.
  • Publish the TXT record at google._domainkey and then click Start authentication.
  • Allow up to 48 hours, per Google's documentation.
  • Verify by checking headers for dkim=pass on your own domain.
  • Other sending tools need their own DKIM setup.

Quick check, no setup: enter your work email on the home page.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be an admin?

Yes. DKIM is turned on in the Google Admin console by an administrator, and you need access to your domain's DNS host to publish the key.

How long does it take?

Google's documentation says it can take up to 48 hours for DKIM authentication to start working after you add the key, though it often takes less.

Which key length should I choose?

Google's documentation recommends 2048-bit if your DNS provider supports it, with 1024-bit available for providers that do not.

How do I confirm it works?

Send a message to an outside mailbox, open the original message headers, and look for dkim=pass with your domain.
