Property managers: protect rent, vendor and owner emails from fakes

A property management company is a financial intermediary. Tenants pay you. Owners receive money from you. Vendors, from plumbers to landscapers, expect payment from you. That means three groups of people look at your sender name before they decide to trust a payment instruction.

What it can look like

A made-up example: on the 28th of the month, tenants in one building receive a message that appears to be from your management company. "Our online payment portal is upgrading. For this month only, please send rent to the account below." The logo, the signature and the domain look familiar. The tenants have no particular reason to question it.

On the vendor side, your accounts payable clerk receives a note that appears to be from a long-time roofer: "New bank details attached." On the owner side, someone writes as an owner to your accountant: "Please send my distribution to my new account."

In each case, an unprotected domain lets the sender line read exactly like yours.

Why management companies are picked

You handle many small payments for many people, and the volume makes each one feel routine. Rent due dates are fixed and public to anyone with a tenant's address. Owners often travel or work irregular hours, and their emails are short. Staff change often, so not everyone knows who the real vendors are.

Your website lists your company address, and a tenant email list may be easy to infer.

What closes the gap

The email system does not check by default whether a "From" address is honest. DMARC is a public instruction on your domain: "If a message says it is from us but fails the identity checks, quarantine or reject it." Large mailbox providers follow it.

To set it up:

  • Turn on DKIM at your email provider.
  • Publish SPF and DMARC at your domain host.
  • List every sender: rent-collection or accounting software, maintenance request tools, newsletter systems, e-signature.
  • Tighten gradually after reviewing reports.

For a property manager, the sender list matters. If a tenant-portal notification is missing from your SPF record and you enforce too soon, real rent reminders may land in spam.

What it does not cover

  • Lookalike domains, like "managment" with a missing letter.
  • Compromised mailboxes at your company, with vendors or with owners.
  • Fake payment links on look-alike websites reached by other routes.
  • Calls and texts.

You should assume that DMARC makes one kind of fake harder, not that tenants will never see a fake.

A word on tenants and owners

Tenants cannot be expected to inspect email settings, so the best protection is a predictable pattern. Pay rent in one place, through one portal, and say so at lease signing and in every reminder. Owners, likewise, should know that distribution changes need a form and a call. When your normal pattern is clear, anything that departs from it is easier to spot, even for people who have never heard of DMARC.

What to do this week

  • Learn who controls your domain host and who administers email.
  • Check the existing DMARC record.
  • List all systems that send mail as your company.
  • Enable DKIM, correct SPF, publish DMARC in monitor mode.
  • Tell tenants once, in a lease addendum or portal notice: "Rent is paid only through our portal. We never change payment details by email."
  • Require vendor bank changes to be confirmed by phone with the number on file.
  • Require two people to approve changes to owner distribution accounts.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in on all mailboxes.

Frequently asked questions

What fake emails hit property managers?

Messages telling tenants to pay rent to a new account, vendor invoices with changed bank details, and notes to staff posing as an owner asking for a payment or a disbursement.

Could tenants be tricked using my company name?

If your domain is unprotected, a message with your company's address in the From line can reach tenants. Tenants often act quickly on rent notices.

We use property management software for rent notices. Does it matter?

Yes. It must be authorized to send as your domain. That is part of the setup.

Do owner funds and security deposits make us a bigger target?

Anyone holding other people's money is attractive. Holding deposits and owner distributions adds to that.

Does this stop a hacked leasing agent mailbox?

No. If a login is stolen, mail from that account is genuine to the checks. Use two-step sign-in.
