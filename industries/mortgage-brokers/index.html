Mortgage brokers: when a fake email carries your company name

A mortgage file is a pile of private paper: pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements, IDs. A borrower sends it by email, usually in a hurry, to a person they have only recently met. That is a lot of trust placed in a sender line.

For a small brokerage, the exposure runs in two directions. A stranger can imitate your loan officer to a borrower. Or a stranger can imitate a borrower, a title company or a lender to your processor.

How it can look

A hypothetical situation: a borrower is waiting for conditional approval. They get an email that appears to come from their loan officer's real address. It says: "Underwriting needs one more item. Please upload your latest statements through this secure link by end of day." The link goes to a page that is not yours.

Another version aims at money. Near closing, a note appears to come from the broker's office saying the appraisal or fee payment account has changed, with a new set of details.

Or a processor receives what looks like a message from a title company with "updated payoff information" for a refinance. Without a call back, it looks fine.

Why brokerages get targeted

Brokerages deal in data and deadlines. Borrowers are anxious and ready to comply quickly. Loan officers are listed publicly with names and emails. Small shops rarely have a security team, and tools such as loan origination software, CRM and e-sign each send mail on your behalf, so settings can be messy.

The details of a loan file are also useful: a message that mentions the right property and loan amount feels trustworthy.

What closes the gap

Normal email will carry any "From" address. DMARC is a public note on your domain telling receiving mail systems what to do with a message that claims to be from you but fails the identity checks: watch, quarantine, or reject.

You need:

  • DKIM signing and a correct SPF record that cover every tool that sends as your domain, including your origination system and CRM.
  • A DMARC record published at your domain host.
  • A staged rollout. Start by collecting reports so you can see everything that sends as you. Then tighten.

The reports step matters for a brokerage because so many third-party tools send on your behalf. If one is missing from SPF and you enforce too soon, your own borrower emails could land in spam.

What it does not cover

  • Lookalike domains that are not yours.
  • Hacked inboxes, where mail is sent from a real, compromised account.
  • Compromised third parties, like a title company's spoofed domain.
  • Fake web pages that a borrower reaches through any link.

DMARC reduces one common route. It does not review links or attachments.

Make the real process obvious

Borrowers cannot tell a real document request from a fake unless they know what the real one looks like. In your welcome email, name the one secure way you collect documents, and say you will never ask for funds to a changed account. When your real process is clearly explained, a message that departs from it stands out to people who have never heard the word DMARC.

This week

  • Ask who controls your domain host and email admin settings.
  • Inventory every system that sends email using your domain.
  • Check whether DMARC exists and what it is set to.
  • Turn on DKIM; correct SPF.
  • Publish DMARC in report-only mode, and set a date for review.
  • Tell borrowers in your welcome email how you collect documents and that you will never ask them to send money to a changed account by email.
  • Use your own secure upload portal and say so, so a different link stands out.
  • Switch on two-step sign-in for all staff.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest email risk for a mortgage broker?

Borrowers trust a loan officer's email enough to send sensitive documents or money. A fake message that appears to come from you can ask for either.

Can someone pose as my loan officer to my borrower?

If your domain has no enforced DMARC policy, a stranger can often send mail showing your address in the From line. Protection makes that much harder at major providers.

Is this the same as encryption?

No. Encryption protects message contents in transit. DMARC is about whether the sender address is genuine.

Do lender rules require this?

We do not give legal advice. Lender partners and regulators may have expectations about email security, so ask your compliance advisor what may be relevant.

Do we need a new email system?

No. The change is a few settings at your domain host and email provider.
