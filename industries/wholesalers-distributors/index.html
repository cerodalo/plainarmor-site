Wholesalers and distributors: protect your invoices and your order inbox

Wholesalers and distributors sit between makers and sellers. You buy from many suppliers and sell on account to many customers. The business runs on net terms, open invoices and monthly statements, and nearly all of it is delivered by email.

That structure is what makes email fraud interesting to a stranger. There are many payment relationships, and each one has a changeable detail.

How it can look

A hypothetical example: your accounts payable clerk receives an email that appears to come from a regular product supplier. "Effective immediately, we use a different bank. Please update our remittance information." The message includes a realistic bank letter. It arrives just before a large payment is due.

A customer-facing version: a retailer who buys from you every month receives your monthly statement, but the remittance box has been altered, and the email appears to come from your accounts receivable address.

A third: someone emails your sales desk pretending to be an existing customer, asking for a rush order on credit shipped to a different address.

Why distributors are picked

You process high volumes of invoices, and a single missed detail can pass unnoticed among hundreds. Customers and suppliers are used to routine reminders and statements. Accounts payable and receivable staff may share a small team. Public records, trade directories and your own website list who handles what.

Distributors usually carry a lot of inventory on credit, so goods shipped to a wrong address can be as costly as money sent to a wrong account.

What closes the gap

Plain email lets any sender claim any "From" address. DMARC is a public note on your domain that says: "If a message claims to be from us but fails identity checks, treat it as suspicious." Large providers apply that note.

To put it in place:

  1. At your email provider, enable DKIM signing.
  2. At your domain host, publish SPF and DMARC.
  3. List every sender: your ERP or accounting system, EDI gateway, statement-mailing service, sales tools and marketing platforms.
  4. Tighten gradually, from monitoring to quarantine to reject.

Your statement and invoice senders deserve special care. If they are not authorized and you enforce too fast, customers may not receive bills, which delays your cash.

What it does not cover

  • Lookalike domains, which are easy to register and used often against distributors.
  • Hacked mailboxes at your office, a supplier or a customer.
  • Fake new customers asking for credit.
  • Phone and text fraud.

DMARC protects the integrity of your own domain. It cannot tell you whether an email from another domain is honest.

Where the process breaks

In distribution, fraud usually succeeds at a handoff: the person who receives a bank-change notice is not the person who pays the invoice. Write down who may accept a change, who verifies it by phone and who releases the payment. Three roles, even if one person fills two of them on a bad day, are better than one inbox deciding alone. DMARC helps with spoofed mail from your own domain; this helps with everything else.

What to do this week

  • Find out who controls the domain host and email admin settings.
  • Check your DMARC status.
  • Inventory all systems that send email as your company.
  • Enable DKIM, correct SPF, publish DMARC in monitor mode.
  • Adopt a rule: supplier bank changes are verified by phone with a number from your vendor file, and approved by a second person.
  • Add a line to invoices and statements about how remittance details are communicated.
  • Require credit checks and a phone call for new accounts or changed delivery addresses on large orders.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in for accounting and sales mailboxes.

Frequently asked questions

Where does email fraud hit distributors?

At payment and credit points: supplier bank changes, fake orders on credit terms, and invoices that appear to come from you with altered remittance details.

We have hundreds of accounts. Can we warn all of them?

You can add a standard line to invoices and statements that your payment details never change by email.

Do sales reps' addresses need protection too?

Yes. DMARC covers every address at your domain, not just one inbox.

Does our EDI or portal protect us?

It covers orders placed through it. Any email that touches payment or setup is still exposed.

Will tightening the policy disrupt statements and invoices?

Not if the systems that send them are listed as authorized senders first. That is why you start in monitor mode.
