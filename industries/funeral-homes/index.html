Funeral homes: keeping fake payment emails out of a sensitive moment

Funeral directors work with families on some of the hardest days of their lives. Decisions are made quickly, and money is often discussed over email: arrangement summaries, itemized statements, payment instructions, and death certificate orders.

A fake email in this setting is not just a financial risk. It lands on someone who is grieving and not inclined to double-check.

How it can look

A hypothetical example: a family has met with you, and the next morning the person handling the estate gets a message that appears to be from your funeral home. "Attached is your final statement. Please pay by wire to the account below to confirm arrangements for Saturday." The statement looks like yours. The domain looks like yours.

On the vendor side: your office manager receives a note that appears to come from a regular casket or flower supplier. "Our bank has changed. Please use the account details below."

A third: a message that appears to come from the funeral director to an out-of-town relative asks for a payment by gift card to hold a service date.

Why funeral homes are picked

Death notices and obituaries are public, and they name the funeral home. A family is easy to identify, and a payment deadline is built in. The people involved are stressed, often unfamiliar with how funeral payment works, and willing to trust whoever sounds official. Funeral homes are often small, family-run firms with one or two people who handle both the arrangements and the paperwork.

Many have used the same email setup for years, and the settings were never reviewed.

What closes the gap

By default, email does not verify the sender. DMARC is a public note on your domain that tells receiving mail systems what to do when a message claims to come from you but fails identity checks. Large providers act on it, and a fake that uses your exact address is more likely to be quarantined or rejected.

To set it up:

  1. At your email provider, enable DKIM signing.
  2. At your domain host, publish SPF and DMARC records.
  3. List every sender: your funeral home management software, online obituary and memorial tools, and billing systems.
  4. Tighten in stages, starting with monitoring only.

The settings do not alter how your staff write or send email. Families will not see a difference.

What it does not cover

  • Lookalike domains that resemble your funeral home's name.
  • Hacked mailboxes at the home or at a vendor.
  • Fake web pages or fake social accounts.
  • Phone fraud and fake callers.

You should still make payment instructions a conversation. A short phone call confirms the amount and the account, and it also adds a human touch.

A gentle way to say it

You can add the payment note without sounding cold. Something as short as "To keep your family safe from scams, we confirm payment details by phone, and we never change them by email" fits well in an arrangement summary. Families tend to be relieved that you thought of it. Say it once, kindly, near the payment instructions.

What to do this week

  • Learn who controls the domain host and email admin settings.
  • Check your DMARC record.
  • List the tools that send email as your funeral home.
  • Enable DKIM, correct SPF, publish DMARC in monitor mode.
  • Include in every statement: "We will never ask you to change payment details by email. Call us to confirm."
  • Take payment by methods you control, such as a card link on your own website, when possible.
  • Verify vendor bank changes by phone using a number from an earlier invoice.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in for all staff mailboxes.

Frequently asked questions

Why would anyone fake a funeral home's email?

Families pay significant sums quickly, under stress, and often by wire or transfer. A fake message with changed payment details can take advantage of that.

Do vendors like florists and cemeteries matter?

Yes. Funeral homes pay many vendors, and a fake supplier note with new bank details is a common pattern.

Will families see any change?

No. Your normal email works the same when it passes the checks. Only fakes are affected.

Is it respectful to warn families about fraud?

A short, calm line about how you handle payments is fine and reassuring. Keep it matter-of-fact.

Does this cover pre-need and insurance assignments?

It covers email that claims to come from your domain. Insurers and pre-need providers have their own domains.
