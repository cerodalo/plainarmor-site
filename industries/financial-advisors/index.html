Financial advisors: a fake "move my money" email can carry your name

For a financial advisor, trust is the product. Clients email you instructions, questions and documents, and you reply from an address they recognize. A message that falls outside that pattern should stand out, but a fake that carries your exact domain can look like part of it.

Your exposure is mostly about requests to move money, and about the speed at which routine instructions are acted on.

How an impersonation can look

A hypothetical scenario: your assistant receives an email that appears to come from a long-standing client. "I am closing on a property tomorrow and need $X wired from my account today. Details attached. I am on a plane, so please email, not call." The client's name and address are right. The tone matches earlier notes.

The reverse is also plausible. A client receives a message appearing to come from your firm's domain: "Per our compliance team, your account verification is overdue. Please log in using the link below." Or: "We are moving client deposits to a new account. Wire your contribution here."

Both rely on one thing: a sender line that looks right.

Why small advisory firms get targeted

Advisors manage significant assets for a handful of people, which makes each client worth more than a typical customer. Clients and staff are named on websites and professional profiles. Communication is frequent and formal, so a fake does not need to be creative. Small firms may rely on one operations person to handle transfer requests, and the pressure to be responsive is real.

Personal details are also easy to find. A message that mentions a client's family or recent news feels authentic.

What actually closes the gap

Plain email lets a sender put any address in the "From" line. DMARC is a public note on your domain telling receiving systems what to do when a message claims to be from you but fails identity checks. Major mailbox providers act on it.

The steps:

  1. At your email provider, enable DKIM signing.
  2. At your domain host, publish SPF and DMARC records.
  3. List all senders: your CRM, portfolio reporting, e-signature, newsletter and compliance archiving tools.
  4. Move in stages: watch, then quarantine, then reject.

That is the technical part. The habit part is a rule that no transfer instruction is acted on by email alone. Your custodian likely has verification steps. Use them every time.

What it does not cover

  • Lookalike domains, which anyone can register.
  • Hacked client mailboxes. If a client's real inbox is taken over, mail from it passes.
  • Hacked firm mailboxes.
  • Voice and text fraud.

Do not let a good DMARC setup replace a call-back. It does not catch a request that is technically from the real account but is not truly from the client.

Teach clients your pattern

Clients who know how you handle requests become part of your defense. In onboarding, explain that you will always call to confirm a transfer, and that you will never ask them to send money to a changed account by email. Clients tend to appreciate a firm that is a little careful, and a request that skips your steps will feel wrong to them.

What to do this week

  • Find who administers your domain and your email accounts.
  • Check the existing DMARC record.
  • Make a list of every tool sending as your firm.
  • Enable DKIM, check SPF, publish DMARC in monitor mode.
  • Write a one-page procedure: every transfer or change of banking details is confirmed by phone with the client at a number on file.
  • Tell clients about the procedure in your next newsletter or onboarding email, so a request that skips it feels wrong.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in everywhere.
  • Ask your compliance officer whether the written procedures need updating.

Frequently asked questions

What is the key scenario for advisors?

A message that appears to come from a client asking you to wire or transfer funds to a new account, or a message from your firm to a client with changed instructions.

Does DMARC replace custodian verification steps?

No. Your custodian's callbacks and verification steps are important. DMARC only makes it harder to imitate your exact domain.

Is this required by the SEC or FINRA?

We do not give legal advice. Regulators have published expectations on cybersecurity that may be relevant, so ask your compliance officer.

Our email is hosted by a compliance archiving vendor. Does that matter?

List any archiving or relay vendor as a sender if it sends as your domain. Then confirm it passes before tightening.

Will clients notice any change?

No. Your normal mail is unaffected when it passes the checks.
